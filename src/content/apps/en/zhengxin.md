Zhengxin (政心) is the Lex family's tool for understanding political thought. It invites you in by understanding rather than by declaring a position: read the sources, question the concepts, trace the lineages, and restate your understanding in your own words. Understanding settles into comprehension cards, grows into a learning map, and revisions deposit as annals of comprehension. Zhengxin shows disagreements and never adjudicates them — it grows your understanding instead of holding positions for you.

## Four loops

- **Locate** — an event, policy or concept description in, reference-frame coordinates out: concept anchoring, lineage positioning, a chain of historical precedents. What the reference frame does not contain is not invented; weak evidence means "no school assigned," only the gap.
- **Perspectives** — steel-man readings from each school plus the structure of their disagreement: weak readings never impersonate a steel man, citations are verified (fabrications dropped over kept), and unresolved opposition is honestly withheld.
- **Genealogy** — concepts and currents as an evolution chain in chronological order: broken links are labelled as gaps (missing dates, spans over three hundred years marked "no reference-frame node in between"), never papered over.
- **Pulse** — issue position spectra: every move carries verbatim evidence; strong claims without evidence are not produced, and if the upstream is unavailable the spectrum freezes with a declared reason.

## Discipline

Understanding, not endorsement: no predictions, no recommendations, no advocacy. Every output passes a guard gate before publication — the only actions are block and grey, and each hit carries a queryable structured reason. The personal layer (comprehension cards, learning map, annals) lives in a separate private store, physically isolated from the shared reference base.
