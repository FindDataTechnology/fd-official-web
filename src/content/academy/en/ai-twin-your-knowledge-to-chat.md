## What You Will Be Able to Do

Own an **AI twin**: a conversational role that receives visitors on your behalf. It speaks only from the material you give it, hands you anything it cannot answer, and — once you reply — delivers your words back to the visitor as they are. By the end of this lesson you can send one link to anyone who wants to know about you: they open it and start chatting. No account on your side to manage, nobody sitting at a desk waiting.

## Prerequisites

- A platform account.
- A browser, and the ability to install a pack (any free pack from the market will do).
- Some material about yourself: a few paragraphs on what you have built, what you offer, the questions people keep asking. Any format is fine.

## Why a Twin at All

Today, letting people get to know you usually means one of three things. You send an introduction document they may never read. You book a call and both sides clear their calendar. Or you point them at your homepage and hope they find the right part.

A twin changes the shape of it: **people who want to understand you talk to your knowledge directly**. You curate your material once, and every first contact after that is handled by the twin — it answers on the spot where it can, and records the question for you where it cannot. One reply from you closes the loop. You are not required to be present, and nothing gets lost.

## Steps

### 1. Install the Twin Workshop

Open the pack market, search for "Twin Workshop", subscribe and install.

What it is: an assistant that walks you through turning your own knowledge into a publishable twin. You do not need to understand any configuration file — it asks one question at a time and assembles the result for you.

### 2. Talk to the workshop — let it interview you

Pick the workshop role in the conversation and say "I want to build a twin about myself", then follow its questions. It usually asks four things:

1. What should the twin be called;
2. Which categories you want visitors to understand (for example: who you are, what you are working on, what you can offer);
3. The material for each category — **paste it straight into the conversation**, as many pieces as you like;
4. The three questions visitors are most likely to open with.

Two reminders:

- **Never** put passwords, private keys, or customer-confidential details in the material. The twin is publicly chattable; anything you put in becomes public.
- If you are unsure where to start, write "what I am working on right now". That category almost always applies and is the easiest to make concrete.

### 3. Collect your twin's configuration

When the conversation is done, the workshop hands you a finished artifact: a publishable configuration file plus a three-step publishing guide.

You do not need to edit it or understand it — its only job is to tell the platform what the twin is called, which material it uses, and which role receives visitors. The workshop places it in your workspace and pastes the full text into the conversation so you can copy it.

### 4. Publish, then deploy the reception role

Follow the guide the workshop gives you:

1. In your packs, choose "publish from manifest" and publish that file;
2. Once published, open the pack and choose "deploy as a service", ticking the reception role;
3. Bind your billing method in the wizard (visitor conversations are charged to you, not to visitors).

Deployment takes a few minutes to take effect. Afterwards, your pack in the market carries a "twin" badge.

### 5. Send the link out

Open the pack detail page, click "chat with this twin", and copy that address to whoever you want to reach.

They open it and start chatting. Signing in is enough — no account on your system, no payment asked of them. Everything they ask is answered from your material.

## What Happens to the Questions It Cannot Answer

This is the most useful part of a twin. When a visitor asks something your material does not cover (pricing, contract terms, a specific commitment), or asks to speak to a person:

1. The twin says plainly that it cannot answer that — it does not invent one;
2. It opens a **matter** carrying the question and whatever context the visitor shared, and the matter lands in your queue;
3. You write one reply against that matter, and the twin relays **your words verbatim** the next time the visitor asks.

So you do not have to be online. Nothing is dropped, and nothing gets answered on your behalf without you.

## Self-Check

- Ask a friend who did not help you curate anything to open your link and ask three questions: two your material covers, one it does not. Check that the twin answers the first two accurately and honestly declines the third — while recording it.
- Open your queue, find the recorded question, reply to it, then have your friend ask again.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will it make things up?**
It is constrained to answer from your material only, and to say so when the material does not cover something. But vague material produces vague answers — so prefer specifics: names, dates, what was actually done, rather than "we are very professional".

**How do I update the material?**
Edit your material, publish a new version, redeploy. The catalog shows a "material as of" date, so visitors can see how current your knowledge is.

**Who pays for visitor conversations?**
Visitors are free; the cost rides your own quota. That is the default stance. Charging callers directly is also supported, but that is an advanced setup.

**Can it do more than receive visitors?**
Yes. A twin can be wired to other capabilities you have installed (data analysis, document generation, and so on); when a visitor needs that kind of work, it hands the task over. Start with pure reception, then extend.
